# 4 Key discussion

## 4.1 TEA review

**Olle Johansson** (OWASP) presented an overview of the TEA specification. **Michael Herzog** (AboutCode Europe ASBL) raised a concern that the language in the spec appeared to lean toward an implication of a financial relationship (vendor) and should be inclusive of open source projects and other non-commercial ventures. Olle clarified that type definitions are a work in progress in a separate document, available at this time [on GitHub](https://github.com/CycloneDX/transparency-exchange-api/issues/384).

The committee discussed publishing sources and generated artefacts alongside the standard (such as the [OpenAPI spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CycloneDX/transparency-exchange-api/refs/heads/main/spec/openapi.yaml)) and will decide where to do so at a later date.

Prior to moving on to approval, **Steve Springett** (OWASP) took a moment to congratulate the team preparing the TEA specification for their work getting it to this point.

## 4.2 TEA approval

**Chris de Almeida** (IBM) posed the question for the committee to approve the TEA specification to be submitted to the GA for standardization in December, and the committee approved.

## 4.3 TEA editorial discussions

The groups spent some time discussing potential editorial tweaks to the document while the normative text is frozen, such as

* Removing example urls and replacing them with IETF documentation domains ([such as example.com](https://github.com/CycloneDX/transparency-exchange-api/issues/382))
* Removing the term “[canonical](https://github.com/CycloneDX/transparency-exchange-api/issues/385)” and replacing it with “valid” to provide added clarity to the reader
* Steve and the secretary general discussed some potential news release channels to promote the finished standard
