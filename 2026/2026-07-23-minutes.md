## Key discussion

### Introduction of New Attendees

Michael Herzog from AboutCode was welcomed to the meeting and introduced to the committee.

### Announcements

### TC54 Website

An update was provided on recent changes to the TC54 website https://tc54.org/. A new “Schemas” option has been added, initially providing access to the schema produced by the Common Lifecycle Enumeration work. The website can also accommodate schemas from other TC54 specifications in the future.

Generated HTML documentation for TC54 specifications has also been incorporated into the website, providing a way to read the specifications online.

### Published Standard versus Working Specification

The committee discussed whether the TC54 website should direct users to the formally ratified Ecma standard or to the latest working version maintained in GitHub. The discussion considered differences between specifications that operate as living standards and specifications where implementers rely primarily on formally ratified editions.

For Package URL (pURL), participants noted that the ratified edition provides a clear reference point, while ongoing work, test material, prototype definitions and proposed changes remain publicly accessible in the working repository.

Decision, for the present, the TC54 website will point users primarily to the ratified standard. The committee may revisit this approach as future editions are developed or if usage patterns change.

### CycloneDX 2.0 – Automotive and Cross-Industry Interest

An update was provided on discussions with representatives from the automotive sector concerning a need for a machine-readable mechanism to document and exchange risks, controls and safety-related information across vendors. Initial analysis indicated that CycloneDX 2.0 may already address approximately 90% or more of the identified requirements.

A gap analysis is being undertaken to determine whether remaining requirements can be incorporated into CycloneDX 2.0 or should be addressed through an interim approach and/or a subsequent release.

The discussion highlighted growing potential for CycloneDX beyond its established software supply-chain use cases, including automotive, medical devices and physical supply-chain provenance. Members noted that this broader interest may create opportunities to attract additional industry participation and expand TC54 engagement.

### TM-BOM / Threat Modeling

The committee considered the proposed TM-BOM work. The Threat Modeling Working Group and Blueprints Working Group had completed their reviews and were satisfied with the proposal. However, the required RFC labels had been applied later than intended, raising a procedural question regarding whether the committee should proceed with approval at this meeting.

Members agreed that there was no material disadvantage in allowing additional review time. The item was therefore deferred to the next meeting.

The committee was advised that a detailed threat-modeling guide, intended to explain the work in accessible language, had also been prepared.

Outreach is planned to threat-modeling tool vendors, security and privacy experts, CISOs, analysts, relevant organizations and GRC platform providers to encourage awareness, feedback and potential adoption. The objective is to communicate the broader threat, risk and control capabilities planned for CycloneDX 2.0.

Members are requested to complete the necessary pre-reading before the August meeting. The August meeting may need to be extended by approximately 30 minutes due to the number of items expected for review.

### Working Group Updates

### TG1 – Transparency Exchange API (TEA) (Steve Springett)

The group reported on discussions held in Vienna. The principal outstanding issue concerns authentication and authorization. The current proposal is to use a separate authentication/authorization endpoint that produces a token for use by other endpoints requiring authorization.

The approach is currently being tested by several vendors. The group hopes to have tangible results before the next meeting, after which the remaining standardization work is expected to be more straightforward.

### TG2 – Package URL (PURL) (Philippe Ombredanne)

Reported two principal areas of work relating to pURL. Relatively minor changes and enhancements to the written standard and prototype definition schema are progressing. Significant work is also continuing on the test framework, where testing has helped identify anomalies and areas requiring clarification in the specification.

Work is also underway on an ABNF grammar. Continued adoption of pURL was noted, including references to increased organizational use and inclusion in other ecosystems and databases.

For the version-range work, the group is developing the first edition and applying lessons learned from pURL, particularly in relation to testing. Work remains substantial, including development of the type-definition schema, test cases, test data, expected inputs/outputs and versioning of the test schema.

### TG3 – Lifecycle / Events

TG3 representatives were not available to provide an update. It was noted that the TG3 conveners should be reminded of the expectation to report regularly to the committee.

### TG4 – Sustainability (Chris de Almeida and Piotr P. Karwasz)

TG4 continues to meet regularly, although attendance has been lighter during the summer period. The group discussed the format of its developing specification and agreed that, while the work remains under active development, it should continue in markdown rather than move prematurely to a more formal specification format.

Once the specification reaches greater maturity, the group will determine the most appropriate format for standardization.

The group is also continuing work on status classification. The current approach is to use the pURL work as a starting point and adapt it as necessary to make the classification more broadly applicable across ecosystems.

No firm ratification date has been established. Participants considered completion within 2026 ambitious given the breadth of the specification and the further iteration and discussion required. Interest in future adoption has nevertheless already been expressed.
